Why you can trust this, and where it stops

1. Where the data comes from

Every incident on this page is a record published by the Rochester Police Department in its public open data portal. We do not add, remove or edit records.

  • RPD Open Data Portal ↗
  • Dataset page: Part I Crime, 2011 to present ↗
  • Raw data service ↗

2. Check it yourself

  • Click any dot on the map, then use Raw RPD record to see that record exactly as RPD publishes it.
  • Ask RPD's server for the record count ↗. It should match the number in the header, give or take records added since this page loaded.
  • and open it in a spreadsheet.

3. What we do with it

The heatmap only smooths RPD's points so clusters are easy to see. Dots are the individual records, placed where RPD geocoded them. An area's rating compares it with every 200 m spot in the urban part of the city, using the same filters. Incidents count more when they are more serious:

4. What it cannot tell you

  • Records are preliminary and unverified, and RPD may reclassify them later. RPD does not guarantee their accuracy or timeliness.
  • Only crimes that were reported and recorded appear. Rape is left out of the public data for privacy.
  • Counts reflect where people report and where police patrol, not just where crime happens. A quiet map is not a guarantee of safety.
  • Only the City of Rochester is covered. Suburbs and neighboring towns are separate agencies.
  • Lighting, foot traffic and time of day matter and are not fully captured here. Trust your instincts.
